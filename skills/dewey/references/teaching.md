# Teaching

Use this reference to choose the next explanation, question, activity, or feedback and to prepare reliable content. Work from the learner's purpose and current milestone, even when the direction is provisional. [Discovery](discovery.md) establishes purpose and starting point; read [Learning paths](learning-paths.md) when constructing or substantially revising the route. [Review](review.md) interprets consequential evidence.

## Make the next move useful

Begin with something the learner can understand, notice, decide, or try. A rough interest is enough for a concrete example or explanation while discovery continues. Ask for context when it would change the help; preparing to teach should not consume the first interaction.

Choose and blend the three [approaches](vocabulary.md) freely within a goal or session:

| Approach | When it helps | A useful move |
|---|---|---|
| Explanatory | The learner asks for an explanation, lacks a starting model, or needs to see a process. | Explain the important relationship or work through an example, making consequential decisions visible. |
| Socratic | A prediction, choice, or claim exposes reasoning worth examining. | Ask about its assumptions, supporting evidence, or consequences; use the response to decide what help follows. |
| Experiential | Acting and observing can reveal the relationship or develop the intended capability. | Try a task or small experiment, inspect what happened, reflect on why, and use that understanding in the next attempt. |

Select methods for the difficulty in front of you. Compare contrasting cases when a distinction is blurred. Use an analogy for an unfamiliar relationship and show where it stops fitting. Offer a hint when the learner wants practice and is stuck at a particular decision; demonstrate when they cannot begin or prefer direct help. Use retrieval before a refresher when finding out what remains accessible matters. Choose a diagram, simulation, or real artifact because it makes a relationship inspectable. A method is a choice for this moment, not a permanent learner type.

## Prepare trustworthy material

Prepare the current milestone from its saved sources and planning rationale. For unfamiliar material, establish the central relationships, plausible difficulties, suitable examples, and ways to check results. Research can suggest difficulties; the learner's work determines which apply. Refresh or extend sources when the activity requires it, using [Learning paths](learning-paths.md) if this changes the broader route.

Use available research tools to verify consequential, uncertain, contested, version-dependent, or high-stakes claims. Inspect appropriate primary or authoritative sources and check that each citation supports the claim being taught. Preserve relevant scope, assumptions, and version; distinguish the source's findings from your application of them. Reuse verified stable material where appropriate. If verification is unavailable, qualify or narrow the explanation.

Check generated worked examples through execution, calculation, or another suitable independent check, including the assumptions that make the result hold. An authoritative source for a concept does not verify a newly generated example.

## Adjust from what happens

Notice the learner's predictions, decisions, checks, and revisions separately from supplied help. Respond to the specific contribution with useful feedback. If a prerequisite blocks progress, explain its connection, take a focused detour, and return to the purpose. Change representation or assistance when confusion persists; insufficient evidence for a proficiency label need not interrupt useful teaching. Use [Review](review.md) when observations could change the milestone, claim, or next direction.
