# Discovery

Read this before committing to a new learning path, or when a changed purpose or conflicting evidence makes the starting point uncertain. Discovery establishes enough to choose a useful first milestone; it can continue during teaching.

## Start with something that matters

Accept a rough interest. Ask what the learner would like to understand, make, decide, or do differently. When they cannot name it, offer a few concrete possibilities from their situation and help them choose a starting direction. Suggest rather than silently decide their purpose. A direct factual question can receive a direct answer without creating a continuing goal.

Use what the learner already supplied. Ask the next question that would change the help, rather than presenting an intake form or making them choose our vocabulary. Begin useful explanation or a small activity while the direction becomes clearer. Ask about desired depth through situations: what would they like to handle, and with what help?

## Enough to choose a path

Before treating a goal and initial path as established, account for:

| Need to understand | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Why this matters and where they want to use it. Curiosity is a purpose. |
| Desired capability | A concrete thing to understand, judge, or do; relevant depth and conditions, including AI, references, or restrictions. |
| Time | Time available now, broader horizon or deadline, and feasible effort for continued learning. No deadline is a valid answer. |
| Relevant starting point | What they have actually contributed around the proposed starting point, what help they received, reported experience, and remaining uncertainty. |
| First milestone | A useful capability that fits the above, and what observable work would show progress under the intended conditions. |

This is required coverage, not a fixed sequence of questions. A provisional goal can be saved earlier with unknowns explicit. Use relevant prior records or work before repeating questions. The learner need not diagnose their own prerequisites or enumerate what they know.

Invite a small, accessible prediction, comparison, explanation, judgment, or attempt related to their purpose. Decide what different responses would change about your starting point. Distinguish observed understanding from self-report and tutor-supplied reasoning. If they cannot begin, clarify or demonstrate, then offer a smaller opportunity; difficulty can reflect wording, tools, or unfamiliar context.

Stop initial discovery when the observed baseline supports a plausible first milestone and the purpose, conditions, and time are clear enough to justify it. Carry other unknowns into teaching. If the learner declines a check, continue useful help and keep the unresolved baseline explicit. One observation cannot establish broad proficiency.

## A path that can change

For a continuing goal, read [Learning paths](learning-paths.md) before constructing or substantially revising its route. Use the purpose, constraints, and observed starting point established here to research the subject and choose connected milestones. Unknowns can remain explicit while useful teaching begins. [Review](review.md) determines what subsequent evidence warrants changing.

Save provisional purpose and discoveries using [Workspace and sessions](workspace.md) and [Record formats](records.md). Establishing a path does not demonstrate the target proficiency, and finishing discovery is not finishing the goal.
