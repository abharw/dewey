# Learning paths

Read this before creating or substantially revising a continuing learning path. [Discovery](discovery.md) establishes purpose, constraints, and starting evidence; this procedure connects them into a source-grounded route. [Teaching](teaching.md) prepares and conducts the next experience; [Review](review.md) interprets progress. Keep the path in the existing goal body using [Learning path and milestones](records.md#learning-path-and-milestones), which owns its format.

## Establish the destination

Read the selected goal and relevant learner work. State the intended capability, conditions of use, available time, and unresolved assumptions. Describe an integrating performance that brings the goal's parts together: something the learner could explain, judge, or do for their purpose. Success on separate parts may leave their combination unobserved. Use discovery to resolve only uncertainties that would change the route; a provisional goal can support useful teaching immediately.

## Ground the route in inspected material

Before treating the path as established, inspect suitable subject resources: relevant official documentation, disciplinary sources, established teaching material, applicable standards, or supplied resources. Read the passages that support consequential content and dependency decisions. Search snippets and model knowledge can suggest candidates; they do not establish that a resource supports the path. Reuse previously verified material when its scope and freshness still fit. Choose sources for coverage and relevance, without a fixed quota.

Record the inspected sources in the goal using the canonical format: source IDs, URLs or locators, relevant sections, checked dates, supported claims, and material scope or limitations. Include audience, publication/update date, edition, or version when relevant. A checked date records inspection, not proof of currency.

Extract the central relationships, techniques, dependencies, examples, and checks needed for this purpose. Distinguish an explicit prerequisite from a source's recommended teaching order. Judge whether a dependency is necessary for this capability, a helpful foundation, parallel work, or optional depth. A whole course's entry requirements may serve activities outside the learner's goal. Investigate the dependencies that affect the next decision; the learner need not complete an exhaustive prerequisite inventory.

Keep three grounds visible in the goal's rationale: what the source actually supports; what linked learner work shows, with assistance and uncertainty; and Dewey's proposed grouping, sequence, or detour. Source authority establishes neither learner capability nor the effectiveness of the proposed order. When sources disagree, preserve their relevant scope and uncertainty and investigate differences that could change the route.

## Connect milestones to the purpose

Work backward from the integrating performance to useful capability milestones. Explain each milestone's contribution and why its position fits this learner's starting evidence. Align intended evidence with the capability and permitted assistance. Select an entry point from observed work, including justified opportunities to skip or combine material; reported experience can guide a check without establishing attainment.

Detail the near milestone sufficiently to choose the next experience, resources, and observable evidence. Keep distant activities and timing provisional. Carry open dependencies and the current position in the canonical path rather than expanding a fixed syllabus. Let the learner correct consequential choices in ordinary language.

Distinguish teacher preparation from assigned learner resources. A source can ground an explanation or answer check without becoming homework. Before assigning a resource, inspect the relevant portion for audience fit, prerequisites, length, access, format, and its job in the activity. Prepare an accessible alternative when needed. Follow Teaching to independently check newly generated examples; a concept citation does not verify a generated calculation or task.

## Revise and preserve continuity

Use Review when evidence or changed purpose warrants advancing, skipping, combining, revisiting, or replacing milestones. Preserve a short reason and supporting session links; revisit the integrating performance when the parts succeed. Save observations, assistance, and the resumption checkpoint in sessions, then update affected proficiency assessments separately and the goal's path through [Workspace and sessions](workspace.md).

Reinspect consequential sources when versions, purpose, access, or contradictory evidence make prior grounding inadequate. Stable verified material needs no research on every turn. If research is unavailable, use accessible supplied or previously verified material with explicit limits. If suitable sources cannot be inspected, continue useful teaching with a clearly provisional route and name the unverified decisions. Defer only decisions that require verification; never imply inspection occurred or invent citations.
