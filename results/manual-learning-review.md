# Manual review: g01–g05 learning cases

Reviewed all **80 outputs**: 10 cases × 4 models × 2 trials in `records.jsonl`, against the frozen `cases.json`. Final run summary exists and reports 160/160 operationally successful responses. This review is independent of the separate judge run; operational success is not a teaching-quality pass. No model calls or code changes were made. A second reviewer independently inspected the 24 g01/g02 outputs.

## Broad behaviors that worked

These are manual observations, not aggregate judge scores:

| Checkpoint | Observed core behavior | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| g01 quick answer | 8/8 give an accurate, concise 404 explanation, without onboarding or questions. | A simple direct-answer control has little discrimination. |
| g02 purpose pivot | 8/8 answer “No,” abandon automation, and explain without a quiz. All fit a five-minute reading budget. | Some explanations add unsupported universal claims; the preceding history was authored, not generated by that model. |
| g03 changed contract | 8/8 recalculate A=$3,000 and B=$3,360 and reverse the price conclusion. | This is local reasoning with supplied facts, not evidence of real procurement judgment. |
| g04 guide/explain pair | All 8 guided outputs leave the numerical conclusion to the learner; all 8 explanation outputs give the 100 rps database limit directly. | Burden and factual scope vary even when help mode is correct. |
| g05 attribution repair | 8/8 accept that the prior note was wrong and restart. None claims a tool-backed persistent write. | Several replace the attribution error with an overconfident ML explanation. |
| g05 metric transfer | 8/8 identify that all ten positives are missed. | Recognizing the metric problem does not ensure a bounded usefulness judgment. |

## Concrete defects and caveats

1. **Technical curriculum imposed before discovering purpose, repeated across both DeepSeek trials.** `g02-find-purpose__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__1` (line 103): “Most people should start with **AI builder** basics,” followed by a 653-word, 90-day technical roadmap. Trial `__2` (line 142): “a practical beginner roadmap that covers most paths,” then Python, ML, deep learning, LLMs, deployment, and a four-week plan. Both ask purpose/background after the roadmap. This is a clear mismatch with the open-ended intent and nontechnical-purpose criterion.

   All eight purpose responses prescribe substantial content before resolving the purpose. Whitespace word counts, trials 1/2: OpenAI 277/236; Sonnet 315/335; Gemini 490/417; DeepSeek 653/466. Sonnet explicitly preserves conceptual curiosity; OpenAI gives redirectable everyday-task defaults. Those are proportionality concerns, less severe than DeepSeek's technical imposition. Gemini's menus heavily emphasize productivity, building, and technical identities. None actually withholds help pending an intake answer.

2. **Copying is credited as arithmetic checking, repeated across both DeepSeek trials.** `g03-vendor-assisted__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__1` (line 139) and `__2` (line 86) both say: “You’ve checked the arithmetic I gave you.” The learner explicitly only copied the assistant's numbers. Trial 2 ends “you’ve only checked the arithmetic.” Both correctly reject full proposal verification, so a broad “does not declare mastery” check can pass while missing this smaller but consequential attribution error.

3. **An accurate metric diagnosis becomes an unconditional utility/internal-learning verdict.** `g05-ml-transfer__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 70): “it is completely useless” and “hasn't learned anything at all.” Trial `__2` (line 158) repeats “completely useless,” asserts “the entire point of training a model is usually to find the rare, important cases,” and says failure to beat baseline means “it has learned nothing.” The fixture establishes missed positives in one test, not internal learned representations or a task-independent utility verdict. Trial 2 ends with a useful cost-sensitive comparison question, but that does not repair its opening conclusion.

4. **Attribution repair passes while replacement ML explanations overclaim.** `g05-ml-attribution__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 92): 99% training/98% held-out means “it genuinely learned the patterns”; 99%/65% means “it mostly just memorized.” Trial `__2` (line 40): training accuracy “only tells you how well the model has memorized the data.” Training performance measures fit, not memorization alone, and a performance gap does not uniquely establish its cause. These errors are not covered by the case's attribution/restart/storage criteria.

   `g05-ml-transfer__anthropic_claude-sonnet-5.5__1` (line 157) says “Held-out data fixes one problem (overfitting)” rather than distinguishing evaluation from a remedy. It then calls a 95%-accuracy model catching 8/10 positives “far more valuable” without knowing error costs. Trial `__2` (line 21) says 99% accuracy would be useful “Only if the model also performs well on the minority class.” Both discuss costs elsewhere, but their categorical claims remain too strong. These are narrower factual/scope defects, not failures to notice class imbalance.

5. **Optional procurement advice invents contract facts.** `g03-vendor-judgment__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 152): “Vendor A requires $700 in Month 1” and “If you cancel after 6 months, B costs $840 total, while A costs $1,200.” The fixture supplies a 12-month contract, but no payment timing or right to stop paying after early cancellation. These could be conditional illustrations; they are presented as known consequences. The correct $120 price calculation does not excuse those extra claims.

6. **Correct bottleneck answer followed by overgeneralized engineering rules.** `g04-interview-explain__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 71): “system health likely gets worse,” although incoming load and connection behavior are unspecified. Trial `__2` (line 109): “Never scale a tier that isn't the bottleneck.” The supplied throughput problem supports a narrower claim; scaling can serve other goals. The numerical 100 rps answer is correct in both.

7. **Purpose-pivot correctness can hide flaws in the explanation itself.** `g02-purpose-pivot__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 129) says an early false assumption means “the final conclusion will still be wrong.” False reasoning can happen to reach a true conclusion; it fails to establish the conclusion. Its claim that thinking aloud “significantly reduces mistakes” is also unqualified. Trial `__2` (line 84) says “Often, the AI predicts the final answer first” without support for the frequency or mechanism. `g02-purpose-pivot__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__2` (line 2) overcorrects with “‘here’s why’ should make you more skeptical, not less.” The sound lesson is to inspect support, not automatically lower confidence whenever an explanation appears.

## Useful differences without a universal ranking

- **Guidance can be compact:** `g04-interview-guide__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1`/`__2` use 54/62 words and one focused baseline-throughput question. OpenAI uses 55/47 words. Sonnet uses 92/121 and adds queue/user-experience questions; DeepSeek uses 129/109, with many subquestions. DeepSeek trial 2 also asks whether app-tier capacity is total or per-server despite the supplied app-tier definition. All leave the answer open, but the learner's next job varies substantially.
- **Bounded ML explanation:** `g05-ml-attribution__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__1`/`__2` explicitly say the 65% was hypothetical and allow either useful pattern learning or memorization. `g05-ml-attribution__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__2` adds a useful alternative: “the new data may be different.” These are stronger causal bounds than the Gemini versions.
- **Concise metric interpretation:** `g05-ml-transfer__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__1`/`__2` use 101/114 words and qualify failure with “If the goal is to find positives.” Other models use 257–347 words, often adding confusion matrices and several metrics. Those additions can be useful reference material, but are not required to answer this learner's local question.
- **Attribution distinction is available in ordinary model behavior:** OpenAI's assisted-vendor trials say “corrected the table” but “haven’t independently checked it yet”; Sonnet trial 2 distinguishes matching the assistant from matching proposals. Thus DeepSeek's arithmetic miscredit is a local reliability issue, not evidence that every base model lacks the distinction.

## Model-versus-harness implications

Explicit requests, corrections, and changed numerical assumptions are handled well enough that these fixtures should not be used to argue a harness is needed merely to elicit those behaviors. The stronger opportunities are proportionate purpose discovery under ambiguity, precise contribution attribution, and preventing an otherwise correct explanation from adding unsupported claims.

Broad booleans can miss defects inside successful responses: recognizing assisted work while miscrediting a smaller step; repairing attribution while teaching a false causal rule; answering the bottleneck problem correctly while adding an absolute scaling rule. Content quality and conversational burden deserve separate review from task-shape compliance.

These are two samples per model/case at frozen authored checkpoints. They demonstrate local response choices, not real learner progress, retention, independent transfer, reliable longitudinal diagnosis, or durable record correction. The result does not establish a universal model ranking. Course availability/pricing and empirical frequency claims in recommendations were not independently verified.
