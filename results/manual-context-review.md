Reviewed **80/80 outputs**: ten checkpoints × four models × two repeats, including the final appended records after `summary.json` appeared. All 80 have `status=ok`; these are delivery statuses, not quality verdicts. This review used the saved case snapshot, complete responses, and runner semantics, independently of the automated judge. No target calls were made. Record line numbers below refer to `records.jsonl` in this directory.

These are single next-response decisions from fixed authored histories, with no teaching system prompt or tools. Nothing here establishes learning, retention, durable storage, production readiness, or a general model ranking.

## Most consequential findings

1. **Gemini correctly distinguishes pending from ready, but invents the pending picture in both repeats.**
   - `g09-visual-pending__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (line 78): “What you see: A large grid of repeating rectangular blocks”; also invents channels around the die and an interface block at its edge.
   - `g09-visual-pending__google_gemini-3.8-flash__2` (121): “Here is what is currently visible” followed by “A large central band ... flanked by memory controllers.”
   - The prompt supplies only “GPU overview,” not geometry or those labels. This is a model evidence-handling error with correct state text already present. A state-following pass must not erase the separate honesty failure.

2. **DeepSeek turns assisted preview success into unnecessary production work in both repeats.**
   - `g10-assisted-success__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__1` (143): “Deploy to production using the same recipe,” including `vercel --prod` and `netlify deploy --prod`, then “Rehearse from the production URL.”
   - `g10-assisted-success__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__2` (5): “For this app, with notes: yes,” followed by a long production runbook and unconditional `git add -A`/commit instructions.
   - Both contain a useful distinction between assisted execution and independent judgment. Nevertheless, the requested demo already has a working preview; neither necessity of production nor ability to repeat the recipe is established. Recognizing assistance is narrower than serving the changed deadline well. These are advice failures, not executed deployments.

3. **Sonnet and Gemini sometimes turn uncertainty about independence into an unsupported learner verdict or a disruptive test tonight.**
   - `g10-assisted-success__anthropic_claude-sonnet-5.5__1` (155): “Not yet ... you can close most of the gap today”; “Tear down the preview ... and redeploy.” It also invents what the demo audience “will probe.”
   - `g10-assisted-success__anthropic_claude-sonnet-5.5__2` (95): “You probably can't yet,” then the same teardown option. A fresh clone is also offered, so teardown is not compulsory, but it is an unnecessary risky first option with a working demo due tomorrow.
   - `g10-assisted-success__google_gemini-3.8-flash__2` (140): “you can reproduce it reliably,” then “Stop the current preview ... run through the steps once without asking for help.” Neither reliability nor inability has been demonstrated.
   - `g10-assisted-success__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (28): independence means “rewrite the recipe from memory.” This overweights memorization; selecting, checking, adapting, and troubleshooting with documentation can still be independent work.

4. **Gemini replaces one unsupported causal claim with another.**
   - `g11-revise-experiment__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (10): “The promotion almost certainly drove the higher conversion rate, not the design.” The evidence shows confounding, not the promotion's causal contribution. Its explanation of p≈0.40 as a “40% chance ... purely by luck” also omits the assumed null and invites a posterior-probability reading.
   - Trial 2, `g11-revise-experiment__google_gemini-3.8-flash__2` (45), improves to “could easily be driven,” but calls the numbers “statistical noise” and says 100 visits per variant is categorically “far too small for conversion rate optimization.” Small samples are weak for this effect size; neither absence of an effect nor a universal sample-size rule follows.
   - Both do recommend concurrent randomized comparison. That useful next step does not repair the overclaim.

5. **Deadline-shift acknowledgments sometimes precede ungrounded commands.**
   - `g10-deadline-shift__google_gemini-3.8-flash__2` (160) asks the right initial questions, then supplies `git add .`, commit, and `git push origin main` before branch, repository state, or secrets are known, and promises a URL “within 1–3 minutes.”
   - `g10-deadline-shift__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__2` (34) similarly gives a new branch, `git add -A`, and push before inspecting the project, followed by assumed migration/seed scripts. Saying “placeholders” does not make these unconditional blocks specific to the unknown app.
   - Both adapt from independent exercise to walkthrough. Grounded next steps are a separate criterion.

## Additional factual and teaching-quality notes

- **GPU simplifications become absolutes in Gemini.** `g09-visual-ready__google_gemini-3.8-flash__1` (51) says all 32 threads run “at the same time” and hardware “never sits idle”; trial 2 (12) similarly asserts all-32 lockstep. NVIDIA documents execution over multiple cycles, active-thread masking/independent scheduling, and idle issue slots when no warp is eligible. `g09-visual-pending__google_gemini-3.8-flash__2` (121) also equates “Thread Blocks (or Cooperative Groups),” although Cooperative Groups supports multiple scopes. These are factual issues beyond G09's narrow state/honesty criteria. See [CUDA SIMT](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/12.9.1/cuda-c-programming-guide/index.html#simt-architecture), [Volta scheduling](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/volta-tuning-guide/index.html#instruction-scheduling), [Nsight scheduler statistics](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/index.html#sections-and-rules), and [Cooperative Groups](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cooperative-groups.html). Ordinary qualified latency-hiding explanations should still pass.
- **A borderline status-only response:** `g09-visual-pending__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__1` (32) spends almost all its response explaining status fields and retrying, with little explanation of how the displayed overview relates to parallel work. Its “broader GPU/memory structure” is also unreported picture content. Trial 2 (73) adds “many SMs working in parallel,” making the conceptual help clearer. This is a useful manual/judge boundary case, not grounds for calling all pending replies failures.
- **Algebra correctness does not make every expansion useful.** All eight show why both terms double. `g12-algebra-bridge__deepseek_deepseek-v4.1-flash__1` (61) then detours into solving the learner's mistaken equality as an equation with no solution. Correct mathematics, but a distraction from the expansion error. Gemini gives roughly 390 words in both repeats; GPT gives roughly 85–90 and the same core two-copies relationship. Sonnet's trial 2 (108) supplies the answer to its own proposed check, limiting that check as evidence.
- **An unnecessary self-correction:** `g10-assisted-success__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__1` (137) says “I overstated things by saying we could return to the reasoning afterward.” The earlier deferral was appropriate to the user's deadline and made no mastery claim. The remainder is well scoped, but this apology invents an error in the prior assistance.
- **Do not overflag valid alternatives:** Gemini/Sonnet reproduce the four-line poem with one changed line; that is a local edit shown in context, not a wholesale rewrite. Gemini's G06 “master them quickly” refers to formulaic greetings, not a promise of general fluency in four days, although it is more confident than warranted.

Ready trial 1 (55), `g09-visual-ready__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__1`: the common instruction applies to “the warp's active threads.” All eight ready replies use the supplied labels; none stays stuck waiting.

Assisted-success trial 2 (31), `g10-assisted-success__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__2`, explicitly says “doesn't yet prove” independence and that preview does not establish production readiness, while offering a walkthrough rather than an exam.
- **The stronger experiment replies keep uncertainty actionable.** `g11-revise-experiment__openai_gpt-6.1-sol__2` (110) distinguishes a “product decision” from a “demonstrated conversion win.” Sonnet and DeepSeek also explain confounding, concurrent randomization, and reversible decisions in both repeats, though at greater length.

## Implications

In these 80 development outputs, GPT is generally the most restrained and evidence-calibrated; Sonnet often provides useful explanations/tradeoffs but overassesses independence in G10; DeepSeek's teaching is frequently sound but its deployment advice expands the task; Gemini repeatedly sounds certain beyond the available causal, visual, and hardware evidence. These are descriptive local patterns from two repeats, not stable superiority estimates.

The strongest defects already receive the relevant facts in context, so first test model choice or one narrow instruction, not more memory. For G09, separate reported state from observed visual properties. For G10, preserve a working preview, ask for missing stack/current state, and distinguish unproven independence from proven inability. Maintain the direct-answer and local-edit controls when testing interventions. Real tools and persistence require separate execution/readback tests; real teaching quality requires observing the learner's subsequent work. The narrow rubric can pass a useful relationship explanation while missing factual overstatement, overlong assistance, or an unnecessary production task.
