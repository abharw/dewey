A slope at one point

Two points give an average rate of change. Bring them closer together to discover the rate at a single point.

This silent animation shows f(x) = x² near x = 2. The secant slope is 4 + h for h ≠ 0. As h approaches zero, the slope approaches 4; we never divide by zero.

Read the visual explanation
  1. Start with points at x = 2 and x = 3. Their heights are 4 and 9, so the secant slope is 5.
  2. Move the second point toward x = 2. With h = 0.5, the slope is 4.5; with h = 0.1 it is 4.1.
  3. The secant slopes approach 4. This limit defines the derivative at x = 2.
